Evaluating · read this before the benchmarks

Does SignalTree fit your workload?

SignalTree makes one architectural trade, and it is not free. Writes are independent of state size and notification is independent of subscriber count — and you pay for that whenever you materialize the whole tree. Two questions decide whether the trade is in your favour. One of them has answers that send you elsewhere.

1. How narrow are your reads? 2. Do you read everything, or undo deeply? 3. What each library provides 4. Which apps land where 5. Where we lose

Question 1

How many consumers are bound below the top level, and how often do you write?

Only leaves are signals. A write goes to one leaf and dirties only that leaf's consumers — everything else returns a cached value at the cost of a pointer check. An immutable store re-runs every subscriber's projection on every emission and filters downstream, so its cost grows with how many things are watching.

Question 2

Do you read the whole collection on every change, or undo deeply over it?

Either one hands the win back. These are the same measurements read in the direction that does not flatter us.

1.90 µs

update + byId()

Per-entity binding. Fan-out of exactly 1.

97.47 µs

update + all()

Rebuilds the array on every mutation — 1.8x slower than a plain array leaf, with no granularity earned back.

~2.5x

behind elf on undo, 10,000 rows

An immutable store swaps one reference. We write values back into per-entity signals — the cost of granular reads.

Don't take the numbers — re-run them

Every figure on this page comes from a harness in the repository, gated in CI so it cannot rot silently. The fan-out and state-size tables are tools/bench-state-scale.mjs; the collection and undo figures are tools/bench-compare.mjs, documented in docs/compare/real-implementations.md.

Known limit: these are Node v24.3 / V8 on a single machine. Whether the ratios transfer to a browser is not established. Treat them as evidence about the architecture, not as a promise about your app.

One of our own comparisons was published backwards once — an earlier revision claimed SignalTree was 20x faster than elf at undo when it was not measuring undo at all. The retraction and the corrected numbers are both kept in docs/compare/real-implementations.md, because a benchmark you cannot see us fail is not evidence.
